How Much Pea Gravel Do You Need for a Patio?

Pea gravel is the easy choice for patios, walkways and garden beds — smooth underfoot, good drainage, and cheap in bulk. The only question is how much to order. Guess wrong and you either run short halfway through the job or pay to haul away a pile you never needed. A quick calculation settles it in a minute with the free Pea Gravel Calculator.

Start with the volume

The formula is simple: length times width times depth, divided by 27, gives cubic yards. Take a 20 ft by 10 ft patio at 3 inches deep. That is 20 × 10 × 0.25 = 50 cubic feet, or 1.85 cubic yards. Pea gravel weighs about 100 lbs per cubic foot, so that is 5,000 lbs — 2.50 US tons. Add the calculator's 10% waste allowance for uneven ground and settling and you need 2.04 cubic yards. One cubic yard covers roughly 108 square feet at 3 inches deep, so you can check any bed by dividing its square footage by 108.

Bags or bulk?

It takes 54 standard half-cubic-foot bags to equal one cubic yard. For that same patio, 110 bags at $4.98 each cost $547.80. Bulk from a local yard runs about $52 per cubic yard — $106.08 for the same stone, plus an $85 delivery fee, for a total of $191.08. Bulk wins once you pass about 21 bags, so bags are only for small beds and top-ups. And keep the depth at 3 inches for patios: past 3.5 inches, the round stones start to feel like quicksand underfoot.

Plan the whole project

Gravel is rarely the only material in a garden project. If you are also building new beds, the Topsoil Calculator works out fresh soil, and the Mulch Bag vs Bulk Calculator compares bagged and bulk mulch the same way. Measure once, calculate each material, and order everything in one trip.
